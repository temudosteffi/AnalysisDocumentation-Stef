1What is capitation?

Capitation is a way of paying a healthcare provider a fixed amount for each enrolled member, per month — regardless of how many services that member actually uses. The word comes from capita ("per head"): the provider is paid "per member" for agreeing to care for them.

The fixed monthly rate is called PMPM — Per Member, Per Month. If a provider is capitated for 1,000 members at $25 PMPM, they receive 1,000 × $25 = $25,000 that month, whether those members visit zero times or fifty times.

Why it exists: capitation moves financial risk to the provider and rewards keeping members healthy and costs predictable, instead of rewarding high service volume.

3Key terms (glossary)

PMPMPer Member, Per Month — the fixed capitation rate paid for each enrolled member.
Member / Enrollee (EnrollID)A person enrolled with the plan and assigned to a provider.
Provider (ProvID)The doctor, group, or entity receiving the capitation payment.
CapVoucherOne capitation line per member, per fund — the detailed "who and how much" record. Many of these roll up into one payment.
Payment (PaymentID)The provider-level record that holds the lump-sum amount owed, built from many CapVoucher lines.
PayCheckFund-level detail attached to a payment.
Fund (FundID)A bucket of money a cap amount is allocated to. Funds are either weekly (paid out) or withhold (held back).
WithholdA portion of the capitation intentionally held back (e.g., for quality or reconciliation) and released later.
Method (CAPFactors)The capitation calculation method the process targets.

4The end-to-end flow

At a high level, capitation moves from member-level detail, up into a single provider payment, and then back out into separate trackable payments:

1. Capitation runCalculates a CapVoucher line per member, per fund (the PMPM owed).
▶
2. Fan-inMany CapVoucher lines roll up into one provider Payment (the lump sum).
▶
3. Split (PostCap)PostCap_SplitPayment sums lines per fund and splits the one payment into one payment per fund.
▶
4. ReleaseWeekly funds are paid on schedule; withhold funds are held back.
The read side of this data (looking up the CapVouchers behind a payment) is surfaced through the Ohio FI web services as a PaymentInquiry that returns a list of CapVoucher lines for a given PaymentID — the classic "one payment, many voucher lines" fan-in.

5Funds: weekly vs. withhold

Each member's capitation is allocated across up to 6 funds. Three are weekly funds and three are withhold funds. This is what lets the plan pay part of the money out over the weeks of the month while holding another part back.

6The rules applied by the process

After the capitation run, the dbo.PostCap_SplitPayment stored procedure splits the single lump-sum payment. These are the rules it follows:

  1. Read configuration. The 6 FundIDs, the target Method (CAPFactors), and each fund's week label are read from configuration (a delimited string in CLM_SP_VariableValues).
  2. Qualifying rule — which payments split. A payment is split only if it is:
    • PayType = 'CAP' (it is a capitation payment),
    • Status = 'OPEN',
    • on the configured Method, and
    • contains at least one weekly fund (Fund 1–3) and at least one withhold fund (Fund 4–6).
    If a payment has only weekly or only withhold funds, it is not split.
  3. Collapse rule — sum per fund. All the CapVoucher lines for a payment are aggregated with SUM(CAPAmount) GROUP BY FundID. Many member-level lines collapse into one amount per fund (up to 6 rows).
  4. Keep-first rule. The first fund keeps the original PaymentID; the procedure just corrects its amount down to that fund's total and stamps its week label.
  5. Mint-new rule. For every other fund, a new PaymentID is generated, and that fund's CapVoucher / PayCheck / CapAdjustment rows are re-pointed to the new payment.
  6. Status rule. Weekly funds (1–3) → OPEN; withhold funds (4–6) → WITHHOLD.
Net effect: one lump-sum capitation payment becomes several tracked payments — one per fund — so each weekly portion can be released on its own schedule and the withhold portions can be held back.

The qualifying query (simplified)

-- A payment splits only if it has BOTH a weekly fund AND a withhold fund
SELECT DISTINCT p.PaymentID
FROM Payment p JOIN CapVoucher cv ON cv.paymentid = p.paymentid
WHERE p.PayType = 'CAP' AND p.Status = 'OPEN'
  AND cv.Method = @Method
  AND cv.FundID IN (@Fund1, @Fund2, @Fund3)            -- a weekly fund
  AND EXISTS (SELECT * FROM CapVoucher cv2
              WHERE cv2.FundID IN (@Fund4,@Fund5,@Fund6) -- and a withhold fund
                AND cv.paymentid = cv2.paymentid);
